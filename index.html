About

I am a researcher in the James Watt School of Engineering at the University of Glasgow. My doctoral work focuses on data-driven and optimisation-based methods for modern power and energy systems, with a strong emphasis on engineering relevance and deployable decision support.

My work combines machine learning, mathematical optimisation, power-system modelling, and multi-energy system analysis. Alongside academic research, I have contributed to industry-linked projects involving ScottishPower Energy Networks and wider UK energy-system innovation programmes.

I am particularly interested in research that connects methodological advances with practical questions in network operation, protection, decarbonisation, renewable integration, and high-density electrical infrastructure.

Research

Data-driven intelligence and optimisation for reliable, efficient, flexible, and low-carbon electrical and multi-energy systems.

AI for Power-System Protection

Learning-based fault detection and classification in low-voltage AC networks using high-resolution electrical measurements and deep-learning architectures, with emphasis on changing operating conditions and low-carbon technology integration.

Renewable Energy Forecasting

Reliable long-term photovoltaic power forecasting through robust data preprocessing, deep learning, generative modelling, and sequence-based prediction across variable weather and operating conditions.

Multi-Energy Systems & Hydrogen

Coordinated optimisation of electricity, hydrogen, thermal, and storage systems under competing objectives including operating cost, carbon emissions, renewable utilisation, and reliability.

Data-Centre Power Systems

High-power AC/DC data-centre architectures integrating 800 VDC distribution, solid-state transformers, photovoltaic generation, battery storage, capacity constraints, and workload-aware operation.

Projects

A two-stage UKRI-supported industry research programme in collaboration with Iberdrola and SP Energy Networks, under Ofgem oversight, translating power-electronic modelling, optimisation, and AI into practical low-voltage network solutions.

Stage I · Low-Voltage Optimisation & Power-Electronic Control

Independent technical work: established the engineering basis for low-voltage optimisation through targeted technology review, device classification, and modelling of power-electronic solutions for three-phase unbalance, voltage regulation, congestion relief, and power-quality improvement.

Core outcome: compared D-STATCOM and related LV optimiser topologies and control approaches, then identified active phase-power transfer and deployable optimisation-oriented control as important gaps for practical LV feeder applications.

Stage II · LVOE: Operation, Optimisation & Intelligent Protection (Current)

Independent technical work: extended the first-stage studies into a practical LVAC engineering programme using SP Energy Networks data, integrating network modelling, optimisation, protection analysis, and artificial intelligence.

Protection analysis: identified limitations of conventional overcurrent protection under distributed generation, bidirectional power flow, and dynamic fault-current behaviour.

Operation–protection coupling: analysed how extreme weather and optimised operation reshape power flows and alter protection risk in complex energy systems.

Voltage-unbalance optimisation: investigated hardware-based approaches for three-phase voltage-unbalance mitigation and their operational implications in LVAC networks.

Intelligent protection: developed hybrid deep-learning methods for LVAC fault detection and classification, progressing the project towards data-driven protection.

Selected outputs: DPSP Europe 2025 and CIGRE Paris Session 2026.
