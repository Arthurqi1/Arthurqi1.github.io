About

I am a researcher in the James Watt School of Engineering at the University of Glasgow. My doctoral work focuses on data-driven and optimisation-based methods for modern power and energy systems, with a strong emphasis on engineering relevance and deployable decision support.

My work combines machine learning, mathematical optimisation, power-system modelling, and multi-energy system analysis. Alongside academic research, I have contributed to industry-linked projects involving ScottishPower Energy Networks and wider UK energy-system innovation programmes.

I am particularly interested in research that connects methodological advances with practical questions in network operation, protection, decarbonisation, renewable integration, and high-density electrical infrastructure.

Research

I investigate how data-driven intelligence and optimisation can improve the reliability, efficiency, flexibility, and decarbonisation of future electrical and multi-energy systems.

AI for Power-System Protection

I develop learning-based methods for fault detection and classification in low-voltage AC networks. The work combines high-resolution electrical measurements with deep-learning architectures to improve fault recognition under changing network conditions, including the integration of low-carbon technologies.

Renewable Energy Forecasting

My forecasting research focuses on reliable long-term photovoltaic power prediction. It combines robust data preprocessing, deep learning, generative modelling, and sequence forecasting to improve predictive performance across changing weather and operating conditions.

Multi-Energy Systems & Hydrogen

I study coordinated electricity, hydrogen, thermal, and storage systems using mathematical optimisation and multi-objective decision making. Key objectives include operating cost, carbon emissions, renewable utilisation, and reliability under uncertainty.

Data-Centre Power Systems

My current work also investigates high-power data-centre electrical architectures, including AC/DC interfaces, 800 VDC distribution, solid-state transformers, battery energy storage, photovoltaic integration, capacity constraints, and workload-aware operation.
